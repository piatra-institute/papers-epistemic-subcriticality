## Question

The seed proposal asks how a society can make demonstrably false belief systems socially non-reproducing without censoring them, and answers with an epistemic reproduction number: keep the spectral radius of the belief formation's transition matrix below one. An adversarial review of that proposal found the reproduction machinery unoriginal, the formal object mis-specified, and one objection unanswered, namely that nothing in the framework distinguishes making a false formation non-viable from making disfavoured dissent non-viable. This paper takes that last objection as its subject. Can an epistemic system lower the viability of refuted claim-centred formations without a truth classifier, and if not, what should it maximise instead?

## Claim

1. **The non-discrimination theorem (the paper's reason to exist).** A process-level intervention is one whose effect on a formation's reproduction is a function of the formation's architecture, meaning its recruitment, retention, organisation-founding, monetisation and amplification parameters, and not of the truth of its claims. For any two formations with the same architecture, every process-level intervention produces the identical multiplicative change in the formation reproduction number. Content-neutral process interventions are therefore exactly blind to truth. The safeguard the proposal relies on, intervene on processes rather than propositions, cannot do the work it was introduced to do.

2. **Two escapes, and only two.** Discrimination requires either a claim-level classifier, which reintroduces the classifier problem the process framing was meant to avoid and carries its false positives, or a systematic architectural difference between refuted and vindicable formations, which has to be exhibited rather than assumed. The paper takes the second seriously and shows that the plausible asymmetry runs the wrong way: refuted formations are the ones with monetisation, grievance identity and broadcast infrastructure, while a nascent true heterodoxy is a few people with no revenue, so uniform friction bites the weaker formation harder.

3. **Absorption is the missing transition.** Every model in this literature gives a formation two fates, persistence or extinction. Real epistemic systems have a third, and it is the one that matters: a claim-centred formation dissolves when its claim-set is taken up by the general infrastructure, because nobody organises to defend what everyone teaches. Continental drift, Helicobacter, and prions did not win by sustaining movements; they were absorbed once a decisive replicable test existed. Absorption is the success mode of true heterodoxy and is unavailable to a refuted claim.

4. **Absorptivity is a discriminating lever that needs no truth classifier.** The rate at which any claim gets decisively tested is a system parameter an institution can raise without knowing which claims are true: cheap replication, open data and methods, adversarial review that a challenger can actually win. Raising it routes true formations to absorption and leaves refuted ones to extinction, so the classifier is the world rather than a panel. The paper's recommendation is that an epistemic system should maximise absorptive capacity rather than minimise reproduction.

5. **Subcriticality does not even deliver what it promises.** With reinforcement, the invasion threshold is not a viability criterion: the linearisation at zero is stable for every transmission rate while a large enough established cluster persists, so an established formation can survive under a subcritical invasion number.

The paper ships a simulation. Every number in the prose comes from it.

## Kind

Formal model with a runnable simulation, so `has_simulation: true` and `claims_target: results.json`. The construct prices a stipulated mechanism; it measures no real movement, and the paper says so.

## Cornerstone literature

- **The precedents that defeat the reproduction-number novelty claim, and must be named as such**: Bettencourt et al. (2006) on epidemiological idea models; Brooks and Porter (2025) on the opinion reproduction number; Diekmann, Heesterbeek and Roberts (2010) and van den Driessche and Watmough (2002) on next-generation construction and sub-threshold endemic equilibria; Sperber (1985) on the epidemiology of representations.
- **The critique the paper must answer**: Yee (2025) on the limits of epidemiological models of misinformation.
- **Thresholds and reinforcement**: Granovetter (1978), Watts (2002), Centola and Macy (2007), Centola (2010).
- **Movement reproduction**: McCarthy and Zald (1977), Taylor (1989) on abeyance, Polletta and Jasper (2001).
- **Social epistemology and its institutions**: Goldman (1999, 1991), Nguyen (2020), Zollman (2007, 2009), Kitcher (1990).
- **The normative constraint**: Fricker (2007), Dotson (2014), Wynne (1992), Jasanoff (2003), Collins and Evans (2002), Rose (1985) on population prevention.
- **Intervention evidence, including the negative result the paper leans on**: Walter et al. (2019), Lu et al. (2023), Hoes et al. (2024) on scepticism as a side effect, and the expression of concern attached to Costello et al. (2024).
- **Absorption cases**: Vine and Matthews (1963), Marshall and Warren (1984), Prusiner (1982), read through Lakatos (1970) on progressive and degenerating problemshifts.
- **The case material**: Paolillo (2018), Pilati et al. (2024), Pahuus et al. (2024) on the heterogeneity of flat-earth participation, which is why the paper declines the seed's sentinel claim.
